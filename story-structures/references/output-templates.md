# Output templates

Adapt these shapes. Do not reproduce empty headings or insist that every answer contain a table.

## Recommendation

**My reading:** One short paragraph about the intended story. Separate interpretations from known facts.

**Primary option:** Name/version, why it fits, emphasis, what it may underplay, required material and tradeoff. Give a brief structural sketch.

**Alternatives:** Up to two plausible alternatives with the same information and the same canon.

**Choice:** One clear question. Ask up to two additional consequential questions if needed; never exceed three in a round. Stop before full development unless already authorized.

## Structural map

| Stage | Event | Objective or decision | Pressure and consequence | Emotional movement | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name of stage | Specific supplied or proposed event | Who chooses what and why | What changes and how it enables the next event | Relevant movement only | Canon / Interpretation / Proposal / Unresolved |

If a cell mixes statuses, label each clause. Never assign “Canon” to an entire row that contains an invented motive. A pending proposal stays pending after it is inserted into an optional draft.

## Synopsis

State selected structure and version outside the prose. State invention setting and any provisional scope. Then write connected paragraphs, using present tense and including the outcome when established. List proposed additions after the prose rather than filling it with bracketed labels. If the ending is unknown, stop at the last supported development and say so outside the prose; do not write a teaser question in its place.

Prioritize the few findings that affect comprehension or the intended experience. Separate deliberate ambiguity from a contradiction. Deliver revisions when the author has requested and authorized them; do not merely describe how to revise.

## Worked map-to-prose example

Example only. All facts in the following brief are supplied for this demonstration, not facts about a user's project.

**Brief:** Short story. Ina restores radios. Her estranged sister sends their late father's broken radio and requests its repair before selling the family house. Ina wants to fix it without visiting. Her sister has the matching component. Ina asks for it; her sister cannot safely remove it. Ina goes to the house, and they repair it together. They do not reconcile completely. Ina leaves with the radio and agrees to call next Sunday. Use Three Acts, Preserve, about 200 words.

| Phase | Event and decision | Consequence | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Setup and first turn | Ina accepts the repair but attempts it remotely. | The missing component makes contact necessary. | Canon; phase assignment is interpretation. |
| Confrontation | Ina asks her sister to remove the matching component; she cannot do so safely. | Ina must visit to complete the repair. | Canon. |
| Final movement | They repair the radio together; Ina agrees to a Sunday call. | A bounded future contact replaces complete avoidance. | Events are canon; emotional reading is interpretation. |

**Synopsis:**

Ina restores radios, but she wants to repair her late father's set without returning to the family house. Her estranged sister sends it to her before selling the house and asks her to make it work again. Ina accepts the task. Repairing the radio gives her a way to respond to her sister while keeping their contact limited to a practical problem.

The repair depends on a matching component still at the house. Ina asks her sister to remove it, hoping to finish the work without a visit. Her sister cannot take it out safely. The arrangement Ina has chosen therefore prevents her from completing the task she has accepted: to repair the radio, she needs to go back.

Ina visits, and the sisters repair the set together. Their cooperation does not settle their estrangement, and neither the completed repair nor their time in the house amounts to a full reconciliation. When Ina leaves, she takes the working radio with her. She also agrees to call her sister the following Sunday. The repair ends with something more limited than a restored family: another conversation has become possible, and Ina has committed to taking part in it.

**Editorial note:** The connection between the repair and limited contact is an interpretation. No new scene, backstory, motive for the estrangement or promise of reconciliation has been added.
